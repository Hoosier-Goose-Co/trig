Privacy Policy

Trig · Last updated September 2026
Short version: Trig has no account and no server of its own. What it reads from Apple Health and your calendar is used on your iPhone, and Trig never sends it anywhere. Only three things go out, each to fetch something you asked to see: your location, to Apple's own weather service; the area around a run whose map you open, to Apple Maps, which sends back the map; and — only when you search for a race — the race name you type, to RunSignup.

Apple Health (read-only)

With your permission, Trig reads:

  • your runs, including the heart rate, calories and route of each one;
  • your sleep, resting heart rate and heart rate variability;
  • today's step count;
  • your height, weight, biological sex and date of birth.

It uses these so your plan starts from what you have actually been running, to show each run's splits and its route on a map, to show how today compares with your own normal, and to fill in your profile so you do not have to type it in.

A run's route is read from Health when you open that run, drawn on your iPhone, and not kept by Trig. Trig never sends the route anywhere. To draw the map underneath it, your iPhone asks Apple Maps for the map of that area, as any app showing a map does; Apple handles that request under Apple's privacy policy.

Health data is read on your iPhone and used on your iPhone. Some of it is kept in Trig's own storage there as part of your plan: the runs it logs for you, and your height, weight, biological sex and date of birth. Trig never sends any Health data anywhere — not to the developer, not to a server, not to anyone. Trig never writes anything to Health.

Trig does not use Health data for advertising, marketing or data mining, and never sells it or shares it with anyone.

You can change what Trig may read at any time in the Health app (your profile → Apps → Trig).

Calendar (read-only)

With your permission, Trig reads your calendar on your iPhone to notice when something might conflict with a training session, so it can suggest moving that session. When an event does overlap a training day, Trig keeps a note of it on your iPhone — the event's title and dates — so it does not ask you about the same event twice. Trig never adds, changes or deletes anything on your calendar, and never sends your calendar anywhere.

Location

While you are using the app, Trig uses your location for one thing: to read the temperature and dew point where you run. That request goes to Apple's WeatherKit service. Trig does not store your location or send it anywhere else. Apple handles the weather request under Apple's privacy policy.

Race search

When you search for a race in Trig, the race name you type is sent to RunSignup, a race registration service, to find the race, its date and its past results. Nothing else about you is sent with it — no name, account or identifier — though, like any request made over the internet, RunSignup can see your device's internet address. The race you choose is saved on your iPhone as your goal race. RunSignup handles the search under its own privacy policy.

What stays on your iPhone

Your training plan, your run log, your profile, calendar notes and your settings are all stored in Trig's own storage on your iPhone, along with a few automatic copies of your plan kept so a damaged file can be recovered. If you set up Strength, your answers to its questions — including any pain, injury or surgery you tell it about — and the lifts you log are kept there too, and nowhere else. Trig does not use iCloud. Like any app's data, it is included in your iPhone's own backups if you have those turned on. Deleting Trig deletes all of it.

If you choose to export your training or save a copy of it, the file goes only where you send it.

No accounts, no server, no tracking

Trig has no login and no user accounts, and there is no Trig server — there is nowhere for your data to go. Trig contains no analytics, no advertising, no advertising identifiers, no tracking and no third-party SDKs of any kind.

Children's privacy

Trig isn't directed at children and does not knowingly collect data from anyone, of any age.

Changes to this policy
